\chapter{Introduction}
Assessing the impact of climate change and developing early-warning strategies is essential for a sustainable future on planet Earth \cite{un-climate}; every year now marks a new record for the hottest ever recorded, the Earth has more excess energy than ever before, and the Arctic ice caps are melting at a rapid rate. To create such an assessment we must understand the climate, not only at a global scale, but on a local, fine-resolution scale \cite{hclim-fennoscandinavia} \cite{precip-temp-downscaling-cnn} \cite{precip-downscaling} \cite{icyalert-article} \cite{review-ml-downscaling}. Currently \gls{gcm} are used to estimate climate on a global scale, capturing the oscillating weather systems of the planet and revealing the general direction of earth systems. But too zoomed out, they often overlook small scale variability; extreme hydrological events are missed, temperatures are poorly mapped, and local wind phenomena are unresolved \cite{precip-downscaling} \cite{temp-downscaling-rnn}. Regional climate models, on the other hand, model the climate on a local scale by  the distance between the data points of the GCMs. These capture the local variations \cite{precip-temp-downscaling-cnn}. 

Instead recent approaches have implemented \gls{sdm} which utilize a machine learning approach to developing output similar to that of the RCMs \cite{precip-temp-downscaling-cnn} \cite{tomasi} \cite{marie} \cite{dynamical-downscaling} \cite{review-ml-downscaling}. Statistical downscaling methods come with several advantages, the most important of which is a significant increase in inference time. However, the field of statistical downscaling methods lacks maturity, as much of the current literature relies on non-state-of-the-art machine learning methods, such as ResNets, VAEs and GANs \cite{review-ml-downscaling} \cite{review-ml-downscaling-resnet}. Only recent approaches have researched the field of diffusion-based \gls{downscaling} \cite{tomasi} \cite{marie}. As such, there is a vacuum in the field, which must be filled with recent state-of-the-art diffusion-based image generation techniques for climate \gls{downscaling}.

In this thesis, I propose a diffusion-based model for spatial temperature and precipitation \gls{downscaling} of the the EC Earth GCM over the European domain. To improve the model design over previous attempts I will implement recent image generation techniques, such as gamma correction and residual targets. Finally I will evaluate the model output by analysing extreme weather events, such as heat-waves, to make sure the model aptly captures the climate variability.
